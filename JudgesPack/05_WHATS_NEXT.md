# What's next, and why it matters

## The person we keep thinking about

Somewhere tonight there is someone who used to sing all the time. In the car, in the shower, at the top of their lungs as a kid. Then one day someone laughed, or a teacher put them in the back row, or they just got older and quieter, and they stopped.

They still hum when nobody is listening.

The Last Frequency is for that person. Not to make them a better singer. To give them back the moment where their voice mattered, where a sound came out of them and the world answered *yes*.

We have watched people put the headset on, nervous, half-whispering the first line. Then a tower of light rises with the chorus, and the whisper turns into a real voice. That change, from "I can't sing" to singing, is the whole reason this exists.

## Where it goes from here

**1. Your songs, not just ours.** The song system is built around a catalogue seam (`ISongProvider`). Every song arrives as a pack of timed lyrics, sections and melody. The next step is licensed catalogue songs, so you can sing the song that is stuck in your head tonight and watch it build a world.

**2. Sing together.** The rival duel already lets two voices claim the world. The next version puts two people in the same world, from two rooms or two cities, building it together in harmony. A family that lives apart, singing one song and building one place.

**3. A voice that grows.** Your saved songs become a gallery of the worlds you sang. Over weeks you would see your range open, your notes steady, your worlds get bigger. Not a score; a memory.

**4. Calm, and care.** Calm mode and no-mic mode are already in. We want to work with music therapists on gentle sessions for breathing, for confidence, for people recovering their voice after illness. The same magic, slowed down and made soft.

**5. Everywhere you sit.** Seated, hands only, nothing to hold. That makes it a fit for a sofa, a hospital bed, a plane seat, a classroom. We want it in every one of those places.

## What winning would mean

It would mean time. Time to bring in real songs properly licensed, to put two voices in one world, to sit with therapists and teachers and build the gentle versions. Most of all it would mean we get to keep going, and that everyone who stopped singing gets one more reason to start again.
